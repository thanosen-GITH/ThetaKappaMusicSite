What is a MIDI VLQ?

A variable-length quantity stores an unsigned integer in one to four bytes. Standard MIDI Files use VLQ values for delta times and for several length fields, allowing small numbers to take less space.

Each VLQ byte contains seven value bits. A most-significant bit of 1 means another byte follows; a most-significant bit of 0 marks the final byte. This decoder removes those continuation bits and combines the remaining data bits into a normal integer.

How to use the online MIDI VLQ decoder

  1. Select hexadecimal, binary, or decimal input.
  2. Enter the encoded VLQ bytes. Compact hex such as CB00 and separated hex such as CB 00 are both accepted.
  3. Enter the MIDI file's PPQ value if you also want the delta time expressed in quarter notes.
  4. Read the decoded integer in decimal, hexadecimal, and binary.

MIDI delta time and PPQ

For a metrical Standard MIDI File, PPQ means pulses—or ticks—per quarter note. Divide the decoded delta-time value by PPQ to find its musical duration in quarter notes:

quarter notes = decoded delta-time ticks ÷ PPQ

For example, CB 00 decodes to 9,600 ticks. At 9,600 PPQ, that is 1.0 quarter note. This calculation gives musical duration rather than seconds; converting to real time also requires the MIDI tempo.

Accepted input formats

A MIDI VLQ contains one to four encoded bytes. In hexadecimal and binary modes, bytes may be compact or separated by spaces, commas, colons, or hyphens. The matching 0x and 0b prefixes are also accepted.

For decimal input, enter each encoded byte separately, such as 129 0, or enter the bytes as one packed decimal value, such as 33024.
